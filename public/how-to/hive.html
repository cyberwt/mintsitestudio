How to find more words in Mint Hive

Mint Hive gives you seven letters in a honeycomb. Every word must be four letters or longer and use the center letter. A typical day has about 36 answers, and you solve the day at the Minty rank, half of the total points.

How scoring works

  • Four-letter words score 1 point. Longer words score 1 point per letter.
  • A pangram, a word that uses all seven letters, scores 7 bonus points on top. Every day has at least one.
  • Letters can repeat, so “noon” or “eleven” count if the letters are there. Puzzles never include S, so plurals won’t pad your score.

1. Hunt the pangram early

The pangram is usually the single biggest score of the day: a seven-letter pangram is worth 14 points. Look for common endings that use several of the letters at once: -ING, -TION, -MENT, -ED, -ER, -LY. If I, N and G are all in the hive, try the other four letters in front of “ing”.

2. Build word families

One word leads to others. Found TRAIN? Try TRAINER, RETRAIN, TRAINING if the letters allow. Add RE- and UN- to the front, and -ER, -ED, -ING and -Y to the end of each word you find.

3. Go letter by letter

When you stall, pick one outside letter and say words that start with it and use the center letter. Then do the next one. Working through the letters in turn finds words you’d skip at random.

4. Don’t skip the short ones

Four-letter words are only a point each, but there are lots of them, and they suggest longer words. TINE becomes TINIER; LATE becomes LATER and LATELY.

5. Shuffle the hive

Rearranging the outer letters breaks the patterns your eye has settled into. A new order often shows a word that was there all along.
