How to hit the target in a number puzzle

In Mint Sum you get six numbers and a target, and you combine the numbers with +, −, × and ÷ until you land on the target exactly. It is the same idea as the numbers round on TV quiz shows, and the same few habits solve most rounds.

The rules

  • Pick two numbers and an operation; they are replaced by the result.
  • Results must stay whole and above zero: no fractions, no negatives.
  • You don’t have to use every number. Each day has three rounds: Easy targets (20–99) were built from three of the numbers, Medium (100–299) from four, and Hard (200–999) from five.

1. Work backwards from the target

Look at the target before the numbers. Ask what it is close to: a multiple of 25 or 50, a multiple of 10, or a product of two of your numbers. A target of 247 is 250 − 3, and 250 is 25 × 10. Most solutions are one big multiplication and one or two small corrections.

2. Factor the target

If the target divides evenly, the factors are a shortcut. 168 is 12 × 14, and 14 is 9 + 5. Check small factors first: 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9 are all likely to be on the board.

3. Get close, then adjust

Aim for a number within 10 of the target using your larger numbers, then fix the gap with the small ones. Keep a 1, 2 or 3 in reserve for the last step; they are the most useful numbers to have left.

4. Use multiplication early

Multiplying two numbers gets you into the right range fast, especially with the round number (20, 25 or 50). Addition rarely gets you past 100 on its own.

5. Make the number you need

If you need a 7 and don’t have one, build it: 9 − 2, or 4 + 3. Thinking “which number do I need next?” is faster than trying combinations at random.

When you’re stuck

Use Undo or Reset rather than starting over in your head. If a round won’t give, the Show a solution button reveals one way to get there, and you can still play the other rounds.
