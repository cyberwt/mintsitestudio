How to solve a 6×6 sudoku

A 6×6 sudoku works like the classic 9×9 one, just smaller: six rows, six columns and six boxes of two rows by three columns. It takes about six minutes once you know where to look.

The rules

  • Fill every square with a number from 1 to 6.
  • Each row, each column and each 2×3 box holds 1 to 6 exactly once.
  • A good puzzle has exactly one solution, and you can reach it by logic alone. Every Mini Sudoku is checked by a solver for this before it goes live.

1. Scan for the most crowded line

Start with the row, column or box that already has four or five numbers. With only six numbers in play, a line with five clues has one gap, and that gap takes the one number that is missing. A line with four clues leaves two numbers to place; check which of the two empty squares each one can go in.

2. Single candidates

For one empty square, list the numbers already used in its row, its column and its box. If five different numbers are used between them, the sixth is the answer. On a 6×6 grid this happens often, because each square shares a row, column or box with 12 other squares.

3. Hidden singles

Flip the question around. Pick a number, say 4, and a box. Look at which squares in that box could still take a 4: if every other square is blocked by a 4 in the same row or column, the 4 goes in the one square left. Sweep each number 1 to 6 through every box this way.

4. Pairs

If two squares in the same row (or column, or box) can only be the same two numbers, say 2 or 5, those numbers are taken in that line. Cross 2 and 5 off every other square in the line. This often turns a stuck square into a single candidate.

5. Box and line checks

Boxes in a 6×6 sudoku are 3 wide and 2 tall, so each box spans two rows. If the only places for a 6 in a box sit in the same row, the 6 for that whole row must be inside that box, and no other square in that row can be 6.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the boxes as 3×2. In Mini Sudoku every box is two rows tall and three columns wide. Check the thick lines before you start.
  • Guessing. When you feel stuck, sweep the hidden singles again for each number. There is always a logical next step.
  • Only checking rows. Columns and boxes give as many clues as rows; alternate between them.
