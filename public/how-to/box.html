How to solve Mint Box in two words

Mint Box puts twelve letters around a square, three on each side. You chain words together, each starting with the last letter of the one before, until you’ve used all twelve. There is always a two-word answer, and finding it is the fun part.

The rules

  • Words are at least three letters long.
  • Consecutive letters in a word must come from different sides of the square. Letters can be reused.
  • Each new word starts with the last letter of the previous word.
  • Use all twelve letters to solve. Fewer words is better.

1. Find the hard letters first

Rare letters like J, K, V, W, X, Y and Z are what make a puzzle tricky. The long word in the solution almost always uses most of them. Ask which everyday words use two of the rare letters together.

2. Think about how words end

The second word has to start where the first one ends. Endings like E, S, T, Y and D give the second word plenty of options; endings like Q leave almost none. Before you commit to a first word, check that some word can start with its last letter.

3. Aim for one long word

In a two-word solution, the words share one letter (where they join), so they need at least 13 letters between them. A seven- or eight-letter first word that clears most of the box leaves a short, easy second word. The two-word answers in Mint Box are always everyday words of five to nine letters.

4. Use the sides as a filter

Two letters on the same side can never sit next to each other in a word. Whole families of words are ruled out at a glance; use that to narrow your options.

5. Solve it, then improve it

It is fine to solve in three or four words first. Once you have, look at the letters your longest word covers and ask whether one word could cover the rest. Your best count shows in your shared result.
