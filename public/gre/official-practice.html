How to use official GRE practice

Only ETS, the maker of the GRE, publishes real GRE questions. They’re the best measure of where you stand, and there are only a few of them, so don’t waste them on day one. Here is what exists and when to use each piece.

The plan: save the official tests for checkpoints

Use original practice to learn and drill, and spend official material where accuracy matters most: measuring your level. This fits our 8-week study plan.

WhenOfficial materialMint GRE
Week 1POWERPREP test 1, untimed, to learn the software and see every question type.Read the format guide; start vocab.
Weeks 2–5About 10 Official Guide questions a day in the area you’re studying. Skim the Math Review chapter for each Quant topic.Practice questions by topic, then Missed only a few days later.
Week 4POWERPREP test 2, timed: your first real score checkpoint.Compare with your mock test result.
Weeks 5–7The Verbal and Quant practice books for your weaker measure. Write essays on topics from the Issue pool.Timed mock tests to build stamina.
Week 7–8The two Official Guide paper tests, or a POWERPREP PLUS test, about a week before test day.Redo every missed question.

Getting the most from real questions

  • Never retake an official test soon after. You’ll remember answers and the score will lie to you.
  • Review every miss the same day. Write down why you missed it: careless, didn’t know it, or fell for a trap.
  • Time yourself. About 1.5 minutes per Verbal question and 1.75 per Quant question.
  • Be wary of “real questions” elsewhere. Sites claiming to host leaked or recalled live-test questions aren’t official, and ETS doesn’t publish its live questions.
