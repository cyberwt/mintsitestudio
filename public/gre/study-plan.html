An 8-week GRE study plan

About 8–10 hours a week for 8 weeks is enough for most people to improve meaningfully. If you only have a month, use the 4-week version below.

Before you start

  • Take a timed diagnostic. ETS offers free official practice tests (POWERPREP). Your starting scores tell you where the hours should go.
  • Set a target. Look up typical scores for the programs you want and write down your Verbal and Quant goals.
  • Book the test. A date on the calendar keeps the plan real. You can retake after 21 days if you need to.

The 8-week plan

WeekFocusDo this
1Diagnose and learn the formatsRead the format guide. Try every question type untimed. Start 10 vocab words a day.
2Arithmetic and number propertiesFractions, percents, ratios, primes, divisibility, odd/even rules. Review the formula sheet.
3Algebra and text completionLinear equations, inequalities, exponents, absolute value. Text completion: find the clue and the signal word before looking at choices.
4Geometry and sentence equivalenceTriangles, circles, polygons, coordinate geometry. Sentence equivalence: predict a word, then find the two choices that match it.
5Data analysis and readingMean, median, standard deviation, probability, counting, reading graphs. One reading passage a day, summarizing each paragraph in a few words.
6Timed sectionsPractice sections under real timing (about 1.5 minutes per Verbal question, 1.75 per Quant). Review every miss.
7Full practice test + Issue essayTake a full timed test. Write two timed 30-minute Issue essays.
8Weak spots and restRedo missed questions. Take a final practice test early in the week, then taper. Sleep well the last two nights.

The 4-week fast track

  • Week 1: diagnostic, formats, arithmetic and algebra.
  • Week 2: geometry, data analysis, text completion and sentence equivalence.
  • Week 3: reading comprehension daily and timed sections; one Issue essay.
  • Week 4: a full practice test, a review of every miss, a second test, then rest.

Habits that move scores

  • Keep an error log. For every miss, write why: careless, didn’t know the concept, or fell for a trap. Patterns show you what to study.
  • Review right answers too. If you got it right slowly, the explanation probably shows a faster route.
  • Practice a little every day. The daily question on our homepage takes two minutes.
